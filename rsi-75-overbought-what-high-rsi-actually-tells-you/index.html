RSI 75: Overbought or Just a Strong Trend?

An RSI above 75 is not a sell signal — it is a measurement of how one-sided recent trading has been. What the number shows, why it stays pinned in trends, and how to read it without acting on reflex.

Short answer: RSI 75 means the last 14 bars have been lopsided enough that gains dominate losses by a wide margin. It does not mean the market is about to drop. Whether that reading is remarkable or routine depends entirely on the regime the market is in — and that regime dependence is the single most misunderstood property of the indicator.

Where do the 70/30 thresholds come from?

Welles Wilder set 70/30 as reference lines in his 1978 work on the Relative Strength Index, and they have survived as defaults ever since. They were never laws. Practitioners running the indicator across strongly trending instruments have long used stricter lines — 80/20, or 75/25 — precisely because ordinary readings run hotter than 70/30 implies when a trend is healthy. The thresholds are conventions for labeling degree, not boundaries between safe and dangerous.

RRK’s OB/OS layer adopts the stricter convention: an OB dot prints when RSI(14) crosses up through 75, an OS dot mirrors it on a cross down through 25, with an eight-bar cooldown between dots. Note the direction carefully — the dot registers the moment momentum enters the extreme zone, not a call that it will leave it in any particular way.

Why does RSI stay above 70 in a strong uptrend?

Because of what the number mathematically is. RSI compares the average gain on up bars against the average loss on down bars over the lookback window and folds that ratio into a 0–100 scale. In an uptrend with shallow, brief pullbacks, the average loss stays small, so the ratio — and the reading — stays elevated for as long as the trend persists. A market can hold RSI above 70 for weeks while continuing higher. Traders who treat every crossing of 70 as an automatic short fight this arithmetic and lose to it.

The useful reframing: overbought describes a condition (buyers overwhelmingly dominant), not a prediction (price must fall). The reading earns its keep as context — a flag saying “one-sided tape here” — rather than as a trigger.

What does a cross back below the threshold actually show?

It shows that the stretch of one-sided trading has ended — the 14-bar balance has shifted enough for the reading to leave the zone. That is a fact about completed bars. What it does not show is what happens next: momentum extremes can resolve into full reversals, sideways digestion, or nothing at all before resuming. Reading the exit-from-zone as a directional signal is where reflex-driven trading begins, and it is the mistake this site exists to argue against.

How should extremes be read alongside chart structure?

Oscillator readings get more informative when they’re paired with location. An OB dot printing while price grinds along the top of a long-horizon range — pressing the highest highs of hundreds of bars — describes something different from the same dot printing mid-range after a drift higher. Same RSI value, different structural situation.

That pairing is the entire reason the RRK overlay puts both layers on one chart: the Donchian channel shows where the cycle extremes sit, and the OB/OS dots note when momentum registers its own extremes. Neither layer tells you what to do. Together they give a faster, more complete description of “stretched” than either alone. The flagship walkthrough covers the band side of that pairing, and the anatomy of a rejection-with-reclaim bar shows the strictest version of structure confirmation.

What this shows, what it doesn’t, and the risks

Shows: when 14-bar momentum has entered a historically lopsided zone (dots at 75/25 crossings), and where those moments sit relative to long-horizon channel extremes.

Doesn’t show: direction, magnitude, timing of any subsequent move, or whether the extreme will resolve as reversal or continuation. It never outputs advice.

Risks to weigh: in ranging markets the 75/25 lines get crossed repeatedly and dots cluster — the cooldown limits but does not eliminate this noise. In runaway trends the oscillator pins near the extreme and the dots describe a condition that persists rather than turns. Any threshold convention (70/30, 75/25, 80/20) will miss some regimes entirely. Descriptions of past bars, however precise, carry no information about future bars — treating them otherwise is the risk that outweighs all others.
